# HTownSim — Houston response prototype

Open `Assets/Scenes/HoustonResponse.unity` and press Play. Engine 7 starts in the middle bay of Houston Fire Station 7 (1402 Elgin St), with Ladder 7 in the left bay and Medic 7 in the right bay as seen from the street. Siren and emergency lights start off. The first fire is at a random storefront at least 150 m from the station (R retries that same incident). Reach the storefront fire, stop within monitor range, and hold E to contain it before the response timer expires. Once the fire is contained the timer stops and you can free roam the city; the siren, lights and camera keep working, and R returns to the station for another call. If the response timer expires first, driving ends until you press R. Free roam ends with a new call: 25 seconds after the fire is out, a fresh structure fire starts at a random storefront at least 150 m away (24 real F1/F2 shops are marked as sites, plus 24 more in the expanded blocks), with a time limit that scales with the distance and the sign, smoke, HUD street and minimap marker following it. Invisible walls keep the engine inside the playable area (x -845..560, z -450..1575, see Expanded city), and the HUD warns "LEAVING DISPATCH AREA" within 30 m of the edge. The sites, walls and `FireResponse` wiring are regenerated by `BuildRealMidtown`.

## Emergency lights

The emergency lights are manual (L). With them on, the engine's full-width cab lightbar, its front warning lenses and its rear corner flashers flash in two alternating groups at 3 Hz, each lit lens getting a soft glow halo that always faces the camera. The two beacons are forward-facing spot lights (red left, white right) pitched down over the road, and red and white light pools are painted on the road ahead in step with them. With the lights off the lenses are dull, so nothing glows by itself. Ladder 7 and Medic 7 are parked props and stay dark. Details were taken from your reference photos: the engine's bumper markers, fog lights, centred plate and end numbers; its rear number panel, stacked tail lights and amber top row; and the ambulance's push bar, five-light box row, corner lights and large "A 7".

## Ambient life and audio

`TrafficSystem` (runtime) keeps the city moving around the player. Up to 80 cars drive the real street grid in right-hand lanes, obey the one-way streets (Austin, La Branch), turn at intersections, follow and stop for each other and for you, and yield to cross traffic. The Elgin Street signals run a 48-second cycle (green, amber and red lenses switch for real) and cars stop at the stop lines. With your siren on, cars within 90 m slow to a creep and pull toward the curb (up to 3.2 m, as far as the street allows), cars within 60 m stop, and they hold for 3 seconds after you have passed. Traffic also will not enter an intersection while you are within 80 m of it. Up to 100 pedestrians walk the sidewalks between intersections with a swing-and-bob walk cycle and step away from the engine. Cars and pedestrians are spawned and recycled within about 220 m of the engine, so the rest of the city costs nothing. Cars do not stop for pedestrians and there are no crosswalk crossings.

## Collisions

Hitting a civilian car at 1.5 m/s or more knocks it onto real physics (`TrafficSystem.TryImpact`): it is thrown at about 1.15x your speed (capped at 20 m/s), spins according to where it was hit, slides, and rebounds off buildings. The engine keeps about 35% of its speed instead of stopping dead, and a thump plays, louder for harder hits. The wrecked car blocks traffic behind it, is recycled after 20 seconds (or after 6 seconds at rest once you are away), and can be hit again. Softer contact still just stops the engine. Pedestrians are given a rigid body when the engine runs into them (more than 1 m/s): they are launched and tumble, stay down for 6 seconds, then are recycled. They can only be hit if you leave the road, since they step away from the engine on the sidewalk. There is no damage, score or penalty yet. Check it with `AgentScripts/VerifyTraffic.cs` (entry `VerifyTraffic.Start`, then `VerifyTraffic.Report` after about 40 seconds); results are written to `Temp/verify_traffic.log`.

Audio is synthesized in code. A new fire call first plays the station ring recording (`Assets/HTownSim/Audio/hfd_mdt_ring.mp3`, assigned to `FireResponse._dispatchRing`; with none assigned it is skipped), then one single tone, six beeps (0.26 s on, 0.22 s off). The siren (Q) and emergency lights (L) are always manual: nothing turns them on or off for you except R, which resets them. Containing the fire plays a rising all-clear chime. Alerts replace each other instead of layering. The EMS tone (a two-tone high/low, eight of each) is defined and ready as `FireResponse.PlayEmsDispatch`, but there are no EMS calls yet.

## Fire growth and spread

`FireSpread` (added to Engine 7 by `FireResponse`) makes an untreated fire worse. Over 75 seconds from ignition the fire grows to 3x: the main flames get bigger, denser and wider, and four satellite fires ignite one by one at 1.4x, 1.8x, 2.2x and 2.6x, alternating sides along the storefront (4 m and 8 m either way). The smoke thickens in step: about 4.5x the emission, larger puffs, a wider plume, faster rise, and a colour that darkens from slate to near-black. Holding E on the fire freezes its growth, and a larger fire takes longer to put out (suppression time scales with the square root of the growth, so a fully grown fire takes about 1.7x as long). Heat, the HUD percentage and the response timer work as before. A new call or R resets growth and extinguishes the satellites; containment stops all flames and smoke. Check it with `AgentScripts/VerifyFireSpread.cs` (entry `VerifyFireSpread.Run`) in Play Mode; it drives growth with simulated time.

## Weather

`EnvironmentWeather` (runtime, object `EnvironmentWeather` in the scene) blends between Clear, Overcast and Rain over 5 seconds. It dims and cools the sun, ambient light, fog and sky tint, emits rain as particles within about 20 m of the camera (columns under a roof are skipped, and drops stop on colliders), and darkens and glosses the `Asphalt`/`LotAsphalt` renderers through property blocks, so shared materials are never edited. Ambience is four looping placeholder clips in `Assets/HTownSim/Environment` (City, Wind, Rain, Diesel) that you can swap for real recordings in the component's clip slots. They are synthesized by `AgentScripts/InstallWeather.cs`, which also created the scene objects and the HUD hint; it refuses to run twice. Ambience ducks under the dispatch tone and siren, is muffled when a roof is over the truck, and is silent while paused. The diesel loop follows truck speed. Authored lighting is restored when Play Mode ends. Weather does not affect driving, traffic or the fire yet.

Verify with two steps in Play Mode, about 7 seconds apart, because the blend takes 5 seconds:

```powershell
unity command editor.playmode start --project-path 'J:\Unity\projects\HTownSim'
unity command script run --file AgentScripts/VerifyWeather.cs --entry VerifyWeather.Begin --project-path 'J:\Unity\projects\HTownSim'
unity command script run --file AgentScripts/VerifyWeather.cs --entry VerifyWeather.Finish --project-path 'J:\Unity\projects\HTownSim'
```

## City sound

Besides the weather bed (city, wind, rain) the city has a spatial layer in `CityAmbience.cs`: engine and tyre noise follows the seven nearest civilian cars (pitch and volume rise with speed), nearby cars honk now and then, birds chirp from the trees and a faint siren passes far off. All clips are synthesized. The layer follows F7 (ambience off), is quieter indoors and under your own siren, and the city bed itself is louder than before.

## Surface textures

Asphalt, lot asphalt, concrete, granite, limestone, brick, stucco and grass use `HTownSim/WorldTriplanar` (`Assets/HTownSim/Shaders`). It projects a tiling detail and normal map from world space in metres, so procedural boxes of any size are never stretched. Each material keeps its own colour and smoothness as a tint, so weather's wet-road effect still works. The detail and normal maps in `Assets/HTownSim/Textures` come from ambientCG CC0 photo scans (Asphalt031, Concrete049, Bricks105, Plaster007, Grass001, 1K), converted to mean-grey luminance so each material's own colour still sets the hue. The earlier procedural versions came from `AgentScripts/ApplyTextures.cs` (run `ApplyTextures.Run`), which can be re-run to rebake them or to re-apply them. Roads use a larger tile (10 m), stronger detail and a lighter base colour than the rest, because detail is multiplied into the base colour and a very dark asphalt hides it from driving height; a large-scale tonal-variation layer in the shader adds patches visible at distance. Weather now gives each road renderer its own material instance for the wet look instead of a property block. Rebuilding the city with `BuildRealMidtown` recreates the materials, so run `ApplyTextures.Run` afterwards.

## City layout

The city is built from real City of Houston GIS data around Station 7 (Midtown). Real parcel polygons become the lots and sidewalks, the gaps between them become the streets, and each real building footprint (2018 LiDAR footprints from the UH layer, matched to parcels) becomes a building. Floors are measured as gross building area (`TOTAL_BUILDING_AREA` from the city parcel layer) divided by footprint area, which covers about 80% of the buildings in the playable window (floors x 3.2 m for homes and apartments, 4.5 m + 3.6 m per extra floor for commercial, 3.6 m per floor for towers). The rest fall back to an estimate from appraised-value density. Only 15 buildings in the whole 1.6 km data area reach 8 floors, so the tallest in the window are a handful of 11-14 floor towers on Main St; the skyline beyond is a backdrop. Roofs are procedural, not surveyed: no source gives roof shapes, so houses get gable or hip roofs along the footprint's long axis (shingle colour, pitch and chimney from a stable hash of the building name), low shops sometimes get a metal gable, flat roofs get a membrane, rooftop units and a stair penthouse on 3+ floors, and towers get setback crowns and antennas. Footprints are from 2018 and the parcel data is from 2026, so newer construction may be missing or mis-sized; the measured floors are averages and cannot show podiums or setbacks. The grid is rotated about 35 degrees so the NE-SW streets run along +Z (north-east) and the NW-SE streets along +X (south-east); the original playable window was x -520..560, z -450..620 metres (about 1080 x 1070 m, 1,206 buildings) with the Station 7 geocode at the origin; it has since been expanded to x -845..560, z -450..1575 (see Expanded city: Station 8 and four blocks north). North-south: Main, Fannin, San Jacinto, Caroline, Austin, La Branch, Crawford, Jackson. East-west: Winbern, Berry, Holman, Francis, Stuart, Elgin, Rosalie, Anita, Tuam, Drew, Dennis. The real METRORail line on Main St is drawn with tracks. The elevated freeway and the downtown skyline are decorative backdrops (the real freeway is about 500 m away). The METRORail line is on Main St, west of the playable window, so no rail is drawn.

Regenerating: `node AgentScripts/PrepareMidtownData.js` downloads the data into `Assets/HTownSim/Data/MidtownReal.txt`; `AgentScripts/BuildRealMidtown.cs` (entry `BuildRealMidtown.Build`) rebuilds everything under `MidtownReal` and moves the earlier hand-placed city, disabled, under `LegacyCanalBlock`; `AgentScripts/BuildApparatus.cs` (entry `BuildApparatus.Build`) remodels the station and builds the three vehicles; `AgentScripts/AddCityMap.cs` rebuilds the HUD map and must be rerun if the world bounds change. The window constants (`MinX/MaxX/MinZ/MaxZ`) live in `BuildRealMidtown.cs` and `AddCityMap.cs` and must match. The street-grid scan in `PrepareMidtownData.js` covers -650..650 m, so a window beyond that needs wider scan ranges and probe lines there, and a window beyond the data download (about a 1.7 km box, fully covered to about +-590 m in the rotated frame) needs a larger `HALF_LAT`/`HALF_LON` first. Run them in that order.

Rebuilding on the finished scene: `BuildRealMidtown.Build` parks every scene root not in its `KeepNames` list under a disabled `LegacyCanalBlock` (`Ladder07`, `Medic07` and `EnvironmentWeather` are now listed), and it resets Engine 7 to a pose in front of the station. After a rebuild run `AgentScripts/RepairAfterExpand.cs` (entry `RepairAfterExpand.Run`), which restores the bay pose and re-wires the weather to the rebuilt road renderers, then `AddCityMap.Build`, then save the scene. The station position it sets already matches the apparatus build, so `BuildApparatus` does not need rerunning. Back the scene up first; it is not under version control.

The station and apparatus are simplified, reference-inspired geometry: Engine 7 (pumper, based on the Houston Engine 1 photos), Ladder 7 (tandem-axle aerial with the "HOUSTON LADDER 7" sign) and Medic 7 (box ambulance on a pickup chassis). `Sample-Asset-Images` contains the supplied apparatus photos, Station 7 and Houston street and map references. Ladder 7 and Medic 7 are parked props with box colliders; only Engine 7 is drivable. Realistic vehicle physics, detailed interiors, building footprints and heights from survey data, and production art are not implemented.

The scene uses URP, Cinemachine, Input System and TextMesh Pro/uGUI. Unity 6000.6 uses C# 9, so scripts use compatible namespace blocks and file-local using directives. Audio is a synthesized placeholder siren with direct volume control; mixer authoring and additional audio are follow-up work. Camera orbit has elevation limits but no building-occlusion solver. The vehicle collider is an arcade capsule, not a full truck-shaped rigid body.

## Expanded city: Station 8 and four blocks north

The playable window now runs x -845..560, z -450..1575 m (about 1405 x 2025 m). It is the original Midtown window plus one block west of Louisiana St and everything up to four blocks north of Fire Station 8 (1919 Louisiana St, about 1.3 km north-east of Station 7, at x -733, z 1091 in the scene frame), ending just past Bell St. Counting from Station 8's own block the four blocks north are St Joseph Pkwy to Jefferson St, Jefferson to Pease, Pease to Leeland, and Leeland to Bell. The new blocks are real City of Houston parcels, 2018 LiDAR footprints and measured floors, like the original city, so downtown towers reach up to about 70 floors; the old decorative skyline is replaced by one beyond the new north edge, and the decorative freeway on the east side is extended north. Fire Station 8's lot is a tax-exempt parcel with no building data, so it is a copy of the Station 7 model placed on the real lot and turned to face Louisiana St (bay labels read 8; there are no apparatus inside). The city map covers the whole window and marks STN 7 and STN 8.

Everything is added to the existing scene without rebuilding it, because the street trees, props, parked cars, traffic fleet and HUD wiring were made by editor passes that are not all kept as scripts:

1. `EXPANDED=1 node AgentScripts/PrepareMidtownData.js` writes `Assets/HTownSim/Data/MidtownExpanded.txt` from a polygon query over the rotated window. It keeps the original street rotation (35.06 degrees) so the new data lines up with the old city (matching parcels land on identical coordinates), pages the city's ArcGIS server by what each page actually returns, finds Station 8 and fits the street grid.
2. `BuildRealMidtown.Expand` (`unity command script run --file AgentScripts/BuildRealMidtown.cs --entry BuildRealMidtown.Expand`) builds only the new parcels, ground, buildings and roofs into sections ending in `_Exp`, plus `StreetTrees_Exp`, `StreetProps_Exp`, `ParkedCars_Exp` and `FireStation8`. Trees, props and parked cars use the same SimplePoly and POLYGON prefabs and car models as the original city, in the same mix, and are kept off the roadway. It rebuilds the street markings, crosswalks, signals and skyline for the whole window, moves the invisible walls, appends 24 new fire sites (48 in all) and the new bounds to `FireResponse`, and rewires `TrafficSystem` in place. It saves the scene and can be run again; it removes its own earlier output first. `BuildRealMidtown.Check` only compiles the script.
3. `AddCityMap.Build` (window constants updated; the HUD slot in `HudLayout` is now 556 high) rebuilds the map, and `AddCityDetail.Run` gives the new shops signs and lots their furniture.

Do not run `BuildRealMidtown.Build` on the finished scene any more: it would park the street trees, props, parked cars and the expansion. A full rebuild would need `Expand` and the later passes run again after it.

The real street grid is a clean rigid rotation of about 0.42 degrees away from the scene frame (north-south streets drift -7.3 mm/m, east-west streets +8 mm/m): invisible over the old 1 km window but 7 m of drift over 2 km. So the expanded data also stores each street's centre and paved extent in the grid's own frame (`Y` line: frame yaw; `G` lines: centre, first and last coordinate along the street, which is where a parcel first blocks its centre line). Street names come from the city's street centre-line layer, voting by length. `TrafficSystem` has `_frameYawDegrees` and per-street extents (`_nsMin`, `_nsMax`, `_ewMin`, `_ewMax`); all axis-to-world conversion goes through four helpers, so the yaw is the only change to the driving code and zero yaw with empty extents behaves exactly as before. Cars and pedestrians therefore follow the real streets, and centre lines, crosswalks and mast arms are drawn along them. Only Elgin St is signalised, as before; only Austin St and La Branch St are one-way (the data has no one-way attribute).

Fire calls scale with distance (`distance / 5 + 60` seconds, between 90 and 300), so the farthest sites, about 2 km away, get the full 300 seconds.

`AgentScripts/VerifyExpansion.cs` (`VerifyExpansion.Start` in Play Mode, then `VerifyExpansion.Report` after about 90 seconds) moves the engine to Station 8, the north end, the west strip and the old city and samples traffic. The last run found about 80 active cars and 100 pedestrians at each spot and no car inside a building in 12,775 car samples (a positive control detected all 40 building bodies it was pointed at). Frame rate in the Editor was 60 to 100 fps at all four spots. Known limits: the grid is rigid, so a few narrow Midtown side streets that bend by a few metres can put a car close to the kerb; downtown parcels are large, so many blocks are single big buildings or surface lots; there are no one-way downtown streets.

## Trees and sky

- **Trees:** every street tree (about 2,470) is a SimplePoly City tree (round, cube or fir) at the original oak position, under `StreetTrees`. The oak primitives are gone, and trees within 9 m of the station bay were removed to keep the cab view clear.
- **Sky:** `SkyDecor` puts the Synty Simple Sky dome on the camera with 40 drifting low-poly clouds. It uses the fog-free `HTownSim/SkyUnlit` shader, so scene fog does not wash it out. Cloud cover, tint and darkness follow `EnvironmentWeather` (clear, overcast, rain). Offset the UV on `Generated/SkyDome.mat` and `SkyCloud.mat` to change the time of day.
- **Props and people:** POLYGON city pack props sit on the sidewalks beside the street lamps, and pedestrians are City People characters playing walk clips.

## Traffic fleet and lanes

- **Fleet:** traffic cars cycle through 20 models: the Stylized Vehicles pack plus SimplePoly City cars, SUVs, pickups, taxis, box and dump trucks, a container truck and a bus. Each is scaled to a real length (`TrafficSystem.LengthFor`), spaced by its own half-length, and has its own collision box, rolling wheels and brake lights.
- **Lanes:** two-way streets keep to the right of the painted center line. On one-way streets (dashed center line) cars now take the middle of either lane beside the dash instead of straddling it.

## Siren control, engine sound, ragdolls and fire

- **Siren control panel (bottom right):** STBY, WAIL, YELP, PIER (phaser), HORN and LIGHTS keys. Off keys are pale and frosted, active keys are dark with a green rim, and a pressed key glows red. Keys: `Q` standby (everything off, or siren on when nothing is on), `1` `2` `3` tones, `T` next tone, `L` lights, and `H` horn (hold to sound, release to stop; it never latches). STBY lights only when the siren and lights are both off. The sirens loop until turned off. The clips are in `Resources/SirenAudio`.
- **HUD layout:** the left side is a layout container: the key list on the far left, and beside it the incident panel stacked over the map. Hiding the incident panel (`I`) or map (`M`) closes the gap and the others move up.
- **Engine sound:** `EngineSound` crossfades idle, 1000 and 2000 RPM loops from a simulated gear/RPM model (revs rise under throttle, fall slowly on lift-off, faster under braking) with road noise by speed.
- **Driving feel and sound:** top speed is 22 m/s (about 49 mph), acceleration 8.5 m/s^2, and steering eases off above 10 m/s. The engine shifts audibly under power (revs drop on each upshift) and has a turbo whine that follows the throttle. Braking (Space, or the throttle held against the direction of travel) plays an air-brake hiss and a tyre/brake squeal. In reverse a backing alarm beeps from the rear (heard up to 90 m). `RearLamps` adds LED panels on the rear: red brake panels light while braking and white reverse panels (plus a spot light on the road) light in reverse. The clips are synthesised in `TruckSounds`.
- **Fire sound:** the crackle of each fire is a 3D source with a logarithmic falloff out to 500 m (full volume within 25 m), played only while that fire burns and fading as it is brought under control, so a fire can be heard from blocks away.
- **Ragdolls:** a pedestrian struck by the engine becomes a physics ragdoll (`RagdollBuilder`), then is cleaned up and recycled.
- **Fire:** the incident fire uses the Vefects Free Fire VFX prefabs (`FireSpread`): big main fire, medium spreading fires, with a heavy smoke column that climbs, widens and drifts downwind as the fire grows.
- **Parked cars** in the lots are the same imported car models as the traffic.

## Truck front lights

## Real-footprint buildings

All 1,512 procedural buildings (shops, apartments, towers and houses) are rebuilt in Blender (`Blender/HTown`, `scripts/build_buildings.py`) on their real 2018 LiDAR outlines instead of bounding boxes: 1,384 match an outline, including 319 L, U and courtyard shapes, and 128 with no matching outline keep their box. Each building keeps the height and wall material it had here. The facades are new:
- **Houses:** shuttered sash windows on every wall except party walls, and front doors on the street side. 949 get a gable or hip roof on their real orientation, with the same pitch, gable/hip choice, shingle colour and chimney that `BuildRealMidtown`'s hash picked, plus soffits under the eaves. The 63 irregular townhome compounds get a flat roof behind a low parapet.
- **Windows:** framed windows with sills on every wall except party walls, with rows on the real floor lines.
- **Shops:** a bronze storefront band on the street side.
- **Towers:** a limestone podium, then either a glass curtain wall or stone with tall windows.
- **Roofs:** a limestone cornice and parapet that follow the outline, and rooftop units and penthouses placed inside the real roof.

Each building's new mesh and its MeshCollider are on an `Upgraded` child. The old `Body`/`Podium` keep their GameObjects but lose their renderer and collider, and the old window strips, balconies, parapets and rooftop boxes are disabled. Chimneys are rebuilt too. The combined pitched-roof sections (`Roofs`, `Roofs_Exp`) are switched off, because every building they covered now has its own roof, including the shops that had metal gables. Incident sites, business signs, awnings, tower crowns and antennas are unchanged. Meshes are in `Generated/UpgradedBuildings.asset`, facade materials in `Generated/Facades`, and textures in `Textures/Facades`.

Regenerating:
1. `ExportBuildings.Run` writes the current buildings to the Blender project.

## Air horn

**H** (or the HUD's HORN key) blows a synthesized air horn; `Runtime/AirHorn.cs` plays it. The engine and the ladder have it; the ambulance doesn't. `ApparatusProfile.AirHorn` (true by default) decides:
- **Engine and ladder:** the synthesized air horn.
- **Ambulance:** its profile has `_airHorn` off, so H gives the plain electric horn (the original recorded `Horn.mp3`).
- **Playback:** a start clip (`Resources/SirenAudio/AirHornStart.wav`, 0.5 s: attack and the first of the blast) hands off sample-accurately to a seamless hold loop (`AirHornLoop.wav`, 1.5 s). The loop plays for as long as the horn is held, then fades over 0.3 s when released.
- **Behaviour:** pressing again mid-fade blows a fresh blast, restart silences it at once, and the old `Horn.mp3` is only a fallback if the clips are missing.
- **Reference:** the horn is modelled on the air horn in video KDFYrhJfku4 at 2:52–2:58.
  - `ArtSource/Audio/horn_analysis2.py` measures it from `Reference/airhorn2_ref_0250-0300.wav` into `horn_profile2.json`.
  - A siren wails through that clip, so it uses a per-frame median spectrum, which keeps steady tones and drops the moving siren.
  - It's a twin-trumpet horn:
    - **Main trumpet (198 Hz):** deep, with the fundamental as its strongest harmonic and the 2nd–4th within 3.5 dB.
    - **Second trumpet (183 Hz):** weaker, with a strong 1st and 4th, and it wanders slightly.
    - They beat at about 15 Hz, which gives the growl.
  - An earlier pass used another video's horn (`horn_analysis.py` / `horn_profile.json`: about 210 Hz, a brighter single note).
- **Synthesis:** `ArtSource/Audio/make_air_horn.py` builds the clips.
  - **Pitch and loop:** both trumpets at their measured pitches, snapped so the 2 s loop holds whole cycles (396 and 366) of each and of the beat.
  - **Tone:** each trumpet's harmonic levels match the reference within about 1–2 dB.
  - **Start and hold:** light saturation, a fast attack that starts ~1.3% sharp, a brief reed flare, a slow level wobble and a little breath noise.
  - **Level:** 3.3 LU louder than the old horn recording at the same volume.
  - **Demo:** `AirHorn_demo.wav` is start + 2 loops + release.

  |---|---|

  | **6 / 7** | outriggers lower / raise (jacks, 0.25 m) |

  - The clip is `Resources/AerialAudio/OutriggerErch.wav`: a 115 ms buzzy "erch" plus 20 ms of silence, so it repeats about 7 times a second.
    - It's made from the supplied censor beep with ffmpeg, at the beep's original 1 kHz pitch: soft distortion, a 45 Hz flutter, and a band-pass around 1 kHz. It peaks at −1.2 dB.
    - The single erch and a 2 s demo are `ArtSource/Audio/OutriggerErch_sample.wav` and `OutriggerErch_demo.wav`.
  - Each source has a low-pass filter at 2.5 kHz (`BeepMuffleCutoff` in `AerialController`) so the beep is slightly muffled; lower the cutoff to muffle it more.
  - It's a WAV so the loop is seamless. The original MP3 is kept in `ArtSource/Audio`.
- **Engine revs:** the aerial's hydraulic pump runs off the engine. While the outriggers or ladder move, it revs from idle (0.08) to working speed (0.50) under load, and settles back to idle when the controls stop.

The tip's master stream reaches 50 m (the ground monitor's reach stays 23 m); with 23 m, raising and extending the ladder put the tip out of range and the water stopped. Restart stows everything at once.
- **How the parts move:**
  - Each part moves along the truck's own axes, worked out in its parent's space, following the rig's drivers.
  - The outrigger beams stretch, the jack sleeves follow, and the hydraulic rams aim at their anchors and lengthen.
- **Export settings:** the FBX is exported without Blender's experimental axis baking, with the rig root pre-rotated, and Unity bakes the axis conversion on import.
